THE CHALLENGE

Dicho y Hecho explores how everyday Spanish sayings can become a collection of books about domestic objects. The challenge was to translate the cultural and symbolic meanings of these expressions into an editorial identity that could be experienced through the book itself.

02 /

THE PROCESS

Each volume begins with a proverb and the object associated with it. In Quien se fue a Sevilla perdió su silla, for instance, the chair becomes a way to talk about presence, belonging and the idea of occupying a position.

I developed the collection through acetate covers, die-cut shapes, monochromatic colour systems and restrained interior layouts. The physical layers of the book are part of how the idea is communicated, not just a way to package the content.

03 /

THE OUTCOME

The result is a connected series of covers and editorial spreads, extended into bookmarks, a diptych, shopping-bag applications and a social media launch. Together, the pieces turn familiar sayings into a visual and tactile editorial collection.
